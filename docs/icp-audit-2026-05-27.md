**Score: 71 / 100.** Solid foundation, three specific gaps pulling the score down: hero/lead doesn't help anyone in the ICP recognize themselves, there's no SaaS-founder testimonial (1 of 3 subtypes has zero proof), and AIOpsForge mentions dilute focus on consulting pages.

## The ICP (restated for reference)

**Founder- or operator-led small teams (1–15 people) who already build.**

Three subtypes:
1. **Bootstrapped SaaS / micro-SaaS founders** — $200K–$3M ARR, 1–8 people
2. **Marketing / creative / content agency owners** — 5–20 people, founder-led, $1–5M revenue
3. **Tech-forward SMBs and consultancies** — small teams with technical leadership, no procurement

Unifier across all three: hands-on enough to want code, lean enough to decide without committee, has the revenue to drop $5–15K on a specific build without it being approval-required. Already understands why n8n breaks at scale.

## The rubric

10 criteria. Each scored 0–10. Total out of 100.

| # | Criterion | What it measures |
|---|-----------|------------------|
| 1 | ICP self-recognition | Does someone in the ICP immediately see themselves on the site? |
| 2 | ICP filtering (negative) | Does the site actively repel non-ICP buyers? |
| 3 | Proof points | Do testimonials, case studies, work examples speak to all three subtypes? |
| 4 | Pricing alignment | Do brackets fit what the ICP can spend without escalation? |
| 5 | Voice / tone fit | Does the language match how the ICP talks and thinks? |
| 6 | CTA alignment | Are calls-to-action shaped for ICP decision speed and friction tolerance? |
| 7 | Information architecture | Does the IA serve the ICP's buying journey end-to-end? |
| 8 | Sample-builds relevance | Do the 10 productized builds map cleanly to the 3 subtypes? |
| 9 | Friction removal | Does the site address the specific objections the ICP raises? |
| 10 | Differentiation | Does the site stand out against alternatives the ICP considers? |

**Scoring scale per criterion:**
- **9–10:** ICP would actively notice and remark on this; competitive advantage
- **7–8:** Solid; doesn't hurt; doesn't strongly compel
- **5–6:** Generic; ICP doesn't recognize anything specific
- **3–4:** Working against ICP fit (mistargeted)
- **0–2:** Actively harmful to ICP conversion

## Scoring

### 1. ICP Self-Recognition — 4 / 10

**Hero copy currently:**
> "Solo operator. AI · SEO · marketing automation. Toronto, Canada."
> Lead: "AI workflows, programmatic SEO, marketing automation. One operator. Built in code, shipped on Vercel."

**About lead:** *"I help solo founders and small teams ship AI, SEO, and marketing automation that compounds."*

**Problem:** This could equally describe a consultant for solo creators, Toronto SMBs, enterprise marketing directors, or YC founders. None of the three ICP subtypes specifically sees themselves. "Solo founders" actively cuts out subtype 2 (agency owners, 5–20 people) and subtype 3 (tech-forward SMBs).

**What would make this a 9:** Hero lead names the buyer types directly. E.g., *"For founders, agency owners, and ops leads who already build."*

### 2. ICP Filtering — 7 / 10

**Strengths.** `/services/` "Who I'm not for" block is excellent. Six specific filter cards: procurement-heavy enterprise, n8n-only buyers, sub-$2K, hourly billing, AGI/RLHF, customer-service chatbots. `/why-code-first/` reinforces by repelling no-code buyers.

**Weakness.** Negative filtering is strong; **positive** filtering is missing. There's no "who this works for" block to mirror the "who it doesn't work for" block. ICP-fit buyers should see a card that says "you, specifically" — they don't get that.

### 3. Proof Points — 6 / 10

**What exists:**
- **Pira A** · Performance Marketing Lead · Boost Collective → maps to subtype 2 (agency)
- **Mani P** · Ops Lead · CISNET → maps to subtype 3 (tech-forward SMB)
- **CISNET case study** → engineering-narrative, well-detailed, subtype-3 proof

**What's missing:**
- **Zero SaaS-founder testimonials.** Subtype 1 (the largest TAM and fastest-deciding segment) has no proof on the site. A bootstrapped SaaS founder reading the page sees zero peers represented.
- Both quoted clients are "Lead" titles (Performance Marketing Lead, Ops Lead) — not "Founder." For ICPs decided by founders, founder-titled proof matters more.

**What would make this a 9:** One SaaS-founder testimonial. One named SaaS case study (or shipped sprint with founder approval).

### 4. Pricing Alignment — 9 / 10

- Scoping Call: free → removes friction perfectly
- Audit: from $4,500 → mid-4-fig, fits all three subtypes
- Sprint: from $4,500 → same
- Custom: from $12K → fits subtypes 2 and 3 cleanly; subtype 1 (bootstrapped SaaS) may flinch but is in-range

**Sub-$2K filter on the "Not for" page** correctly excludes too-small buyers. The pricing structure actively serves the ICP.

**Minor nit:** Pricing brackets visible but the speed-of-quote commitment ("24-hour written quote") is buried in FAQ. ICP cares about that — surface it.

### 5. Voice / Tone Fit — 9 / 10

The site now reads direct, declarative, code-first. Vocabulary is ICP-native: MCP, Vercel cron, schema.org, llms.txt, sub-agents, HMAC verification, idempotency keys.

The `/why-code-first/` manifesto reads engineering-narrative. CISNET case study reads engineering-narrative. Stack page documents tools matter-of-factly. All ICP-aligned voice.

**Minor weakness:** A few residual tagline-y moments survive ("Built like an operator. Sold by the project."). SaaS founders allergic to marketing-language may bounce off these. Reads slightly more "agency owner" than "indie SaaS founder" in tone. Acceptable trade-off given subtype 2 is real, but worth noting.

### 6. CTA Alignment — 7 / 10

**What works:**
- 30-min call is the right primary CTA (ICP decides fast, wants live conversation)
- Contact page offers email + Cal.com + form + DM (good optionality)
- After last commit, CTAs aren't repeated 8 times — one per page is correct

**What's missing:**
- The "24-hour written quote" commitment is buried in the FAQ. This is one of the highest-converting friction-removers for ICP buyers and should appear in the hero or services tier card foot.
- No async-first option promoted equally. ICP includes founders in different timezones who'd rather submit a written brief than book a call. The brief form exists on `/contact/` but isn't visible as an alternative path from the homepage.

### 7. Information Architecture — 6 / 10

**Flow:** hero → numbers band → ticker → services → testimonials → process → work table → FAQ → contact band.

**What's wrong:**
- **Numbers band reads thin.** "1 case study + pipeline" is the first cell. SaaS founders skim "1" and bounce. The other cells lean on AIOpsForge metrics ("51 URLs live") and commits ("30/30d") which are real but read as "this person does community stuff too" rather than "this person has shipped client work." Mixed signals.
- **AIOpsForge mentions throughout the consulting site** dilute focus. The community is real, but on a consulting page it reads as "side hustle." Either compartmentalize it more, or sharpen the wording to make it explicit ("Adjacent project: AIOpsForge.").
- **Process section is generic.** Step 01 → 04 reads like every other consulting site's process strip. Cut or replace with something specific.

**What works:**
- Services grid is logical, four cards, clear pricing brackets
- FAQ structure is appropriately placed near contact band

### 9. Friction Removal — 8 / 10

**Strong.** The site addresses most ICP objections head-on:
- "Will they own the code?" → "You own the code outright" ✓
- "What stack?" → Explicit on every page ✓
- "Scope creep?" → "Fixed scope. Fixed price." ✓
- "Vendor lock-in?" → /why-code-first/ manifesto ✓
- "Timeline?" → "48-hour sprint" or "12-week custom" ✓
- "Solo risk?" → FAQ "refund clause + escrow if I'm out" ✓
- "Stack compatibility?" → FAQ #4 ✓
- "Timezone?" → FAQ #5 ✓

**Missing for a 10:**
- No "first-call agenda" published. ICP buyers want to know what they're walking into. A small block on `/contact/` listing "what we cover in 30 minutes" pre-arms the call.
- No "what data you'll need" pre-publishing for Audits. ICP appreciates being prepared.

### 10. Differentiation — 8 / 10

**Strong vertical differentiation:**
- Code-first vs n8n / Make / Zapier agencies → /why-code-first/ owns this
- $0 platform fee math → specific, defensible
- Public GitHub activity displayed → "30 commits / 30d" signals real shipping
- Open-source skill library (personalClaudeSkills) → credibility unlike a no-name freelancer
- Productized sprints vs hourly Upwork → speed and price-predictability

**Weak person-level differentiation:**
- Doesn't strongly answer "why you specifically vs the other Toronto solo Claude Code consultant." The operator persona is shared by many in 2026. Specific anchors that would help: Anthropic-skill-bundle contributions, named open-source merges, conference talks, prior employer brands.

## Improvements (prioritized)

### Tier A — high impact, low effort (ship this week)

**A1. Sharpen the homepage hero lead.**
Current: *"AI workflows, programmatic SEO, marketing automation. One operator. Built in code, shipped on Vercel."*
Proposed: *"For founders, agency owners, and ops leads who already build. AI workflows, SEO, and marketing automation shipped in code on Vercel."*
Why: lifts criterion 1 from 4 → 8. Single highest-leverage change.

**A2. Drop CDAP/IRAP grant block on `/services/`.**
Already discussed. Markup parked in `docs/future-plans.md`. Pulls positioning away from Canadian SMB grant-chasers and toward the tech-forward ICP. Lifts criterion 1 by ~1 pt.

**A3. Add positive ICP filter block on `/services/`.**
Mirror the "Who I'm not for" block with a smaller "Who this works for" block above it. Three cards: SaaS founders / agency owners / tech-forward ops leads. Lifts criterion 2 from 7 → 9.

**A4. Update About page bio.**
*"I help solo founders and small teams ship..."* → *"I help founders, agency owners, and ops leads ship..."* One sentence change. Lifts criterion 1.

**A5. Surface "24-hour written quote" commitment in hero or services band.**
Currently buried in FAQ. ICP cares about this. Move to a visible micro under the CTA buttons or into the services intro paragraph. Lifts criterion 6 from 7 → 9.

**A6. Add a "Creative Variants Generator" sample build.**
Closes the obvious agency gap. One brief → N variants with brand-tone guardrails. Slot it as build #11 or replace one of the existing 10. Lifts criterion 8 from 7 → 8.

### Tier B — medium impact, low–medium effort (this month)

**B1. Replace the numbers band's "1 case study + pipeline" cell.**
Currently signals "only one client" to a SaaS founder skimming. Replace with a metric that's stronger for ICP confidence: "200+ pSEO pages live (CISNET)", "98+ Lighthouse production score (CISNET)", "12-week solo migration shipped." Lifts criterion 7.

**B2. Compartmentalize AIOpsForge mentions on consulting pages.**
On `/work/`, the AIOpsForge card is fine. On `/services/`, `/ai/`, and vertical pages, references should be tagged explicitly as "Adjacent project: AIOpsForge" or moved out. Currently dilutes the consulting focus. Lifts criterion 7 and 10.

**B3. Tag each of the 10 sample builds by ICP subtype.**
Small chip on each build card: `SAAS` / `AGENCY` / `SMB` (or combinations). Helps ICP buyers self-identify. Lifts criterion 1 and 8.

**B4. Add "first-call agenda" block on `/contact/`.**
Four bullets: what we cover in 30 minutes. Pre-arms the buyer. Lifts criterion 9 from 8 → 9.

**B5. Reframe the testimonials section subhead.**
Current: *"Words from clients."*
Proposed: *"From agency leads, ops teams, and the operators they hire to build."*
Makes the subtype-fit explicit. Lifts criterion 3 by ~0.5 pt (real lift requires actual SaaS testimonial).

**B6. Cut or sharpen the "process" section on homepage.**
Generic 4-step strip. Either cut it entirely, or replace with something ICP-specific (e.g., "what your first week looks like" with concrete artifacts: spec doc, Slack channel, first commit, milestone demo).

### Tier C — high impact, high effort (next quarter)

**C1. Land a SaaS-founder testimonial.**
Outreach work, not a copy edit. The most impactful single missing piece. Approach: offer a Sprint at founding-rate ($1,500–$2,500) to a bootstrapped SaaS founder you respect (5–10 candidate list), in exchange for permission to publish a case study + testimonial. One placement lifts criterion 3 from 6 → 9.

**C2. Publish a SaaS-shaped case study.**
After C1 ships. Same engineering-narrative format as CISNET. Different subtype, different story. Doubles the proof-point coverage.

**C3. Add specific person-level differentiation anchors.**
What makes this consultancy different from another solo Claude Code expert? Options to develop and surface:
- Open-source contribution count (PRs merged into widely-used tools)
- Conference talks given
- Prior employer brands (Ericsson, Imprimo, Fixvi already on /about — could be more prominent on homepage)
- Public Anthropic ecosystem contributions
Lifts criterion 10 from 8 → 9.

**C4. /writing/ → ship 3 essays in the SaaS-operator / agency-operator vein.**
The CPR essay drafting is the start. Topics aligned to ICP: "What I ship for SaaS founders in a 48-hour sprint," "Why I won't build your agency workflow in n8n," etc. Builds long-term ICP-magnet via search + AI search visibility.

### Tier D — small refinements (nice-to-have)

**D1.** Update the "From the operators they hire" framing in `<title>` and meta descriptions to consistently mention SaaS / agency / SMB across the site.

**D2.** On `/work/case-studies/cisnet/`, add a one-paragraph bridge near the bottom: "If you're a SaaS founder or agency owner with a similar shaped problem, the migration pattern maps cleanly." Generalizes the case study.

**D3.** Add a "currently accepting" banner at the very top of `/contact/` that signals capacity. ICP appreciates being told they're not on a 6-month waitlist.

**D4.** Cut "Built like an operator. Sold by the project." from hero on next pass if testing shows SaaS founders react allergically. Keep otherwise — it's signature.

**D5.** Make Footer `BUILDING IN PUBLIC · REPLIES IN 24 HRS` link to a specific public artifact (GitHub repo, or the writing index). Currently aspirational, could become evidence.
