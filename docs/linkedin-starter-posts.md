## LI-S5 · How I structure engagements (and why)

**Format:** Text-only · ~1,000 chars
**Slot:** Week 2, Thursday

```
Most consulting goes one of two ways: hourly billable, or vague retainer with no clear deliverables. Both pull toward Slack-access-as-product.

My engagements run differently:

· Free 30-min scoping call. Walk through the problem. Decide whether AI is even the fix.
· Fixed-scope week if the problem is "where does AI fit?" — written report + ranked opportunity list at the end.
· Fixed-scope build if the workflow fits in one sentence. Monday spec, Friday demo, Wednesday shipped.
· Custom build for multi-workflow systems. Milestone-paid. Daily updates.
· Ops retainer only AFTER something is shipped. Capped hours, defined deliverables.

Every engagement has a written spec before signing. No hourly billing. No "we'll get there." No subcontracting.

I'm not trying to scale into an agency. The whole point is one operator with the right stack ships what teams of five used to.
```

## LI-S6 · First four months as a freelance AI engineer

**Format:** Text-only · ~1,000 chars
**Slot:** Week 3, Tuesday

```
Four months freelance. Honest snapshot:

· The hardest part isn't the work. It's deciding which scope to take and which to refuse.
· Async + written spec wins every time I default to it. Live calls feel productive but rarely move the deliverable.
· Code-first vs no-code matters more to me than to the client. Most clients don't care if it's Zapier or Vercel cron — they care that it works. Education is part of the job.
· Cold outreach without prior trust is brutal. Warm intros from past colleagues are 10x better.
· pSEO works. AI Overviews matter. Schema.org earns its place. The CISNET data is real.

Posting weekly from here. Tactical mostly, opinion occasionally. The roadmap is the work — you can watch it ship.
```
